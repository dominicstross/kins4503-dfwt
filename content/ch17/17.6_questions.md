# Section 17.6: Step 6: Volume — Assessment Questions

## Part 1: Volume Terminology, Volume-Load Calculations, and Work Equivalencies

### Multiple-Choice Questions (MCQs)

1. What is the fundamental definition of volume in resistance training program design?  
A. The total duration of rest intervals accumulated during a training session  
B. The total amount of weight lifted in a workout session  
C. The peak movement velocity achieved during concentric muscle actions  
D. The percentage of 1RM assigned for a primary structural exercise  

**Answer: B. The total amount of weight lifted in a workout session**  
**Rationale:** Volume relates specifically to the total amount of weight lifted during a training session.

2. How is volume-load calculated for a set or training session?  
A. Adding total sets to total repetitions and dividing by load intensity  
B. Multiplying the number of sets by repetitions per set, then multiplying by the weight lifted per repetition  
C. Multiplying repetitions per set by rest period duration in seconds  
D. Dividing total repetitions by vertical displacement distance  

**Answer: B. Multiplying the number of sets by repetitions per set, then multiplying by the weight lifted per repetition**  
**Rationale:** Volume-load is calculated by multiplying total sets by repetitions per set, then multiplying by the weight lifted per repetition (Sets x Reps x Weight).

3. If an athlete performs 2 sets of 10 repetitions with 50 pounds, what is the calculated volume-load for that exercise?  
A. 100 pounds  
B. 500 pounds  
C. 1,000 pounds  
D. 2,000 pounds  

**Answer: C. 1,000 pounds**  
**Rationale:** Volume-load = 2 sets x 10 reps x 50 lbs = 1,000 lbs (454 kg).

4. What additional factor would convert volume-load into pure concentric mechanical work?  
A. Inter-set rest period duration  
B. Vertical displacement distance of the weight during each repetition  
C. Barbell shaft grip diameter  
D. Peak heart rate response during the set  

**Answer: B. Vertical displacement distance of the weight during each repetition**  
**Rationale:** Multiplying volume-load by the vertical displacement distance of the load during each repetition yields the concentric mechanical work performed.

5. How is the average weight lifted per repetition (a practical proxy for training intensity and work quality) calculated?  
A. Dividing volume-load by repetition-volume  
B. Multiplying volume-load by total rest time  
C. Subtracting repetition-volume from total sets  
D. Dividing total sets by 1RM load  

**Answer: A. Dividing volume-load by repetition-volume**  
**Rationale:** Dividing total volume-load by repetition-volume yields the average weight lifted per repetition, which is directly related to intensity or the quality of work.

6. In running exercise, what volume metric is functionally comparable to repetition-volume in resistance exercise?  
A. Stride frequency per minute  
B. Total distance covered  
C. Ground contact time per footfall  
D. Lactate threshold velocity  

**Answer: B. Total distance covered**  
**Rationale:** In running exercise, the common volume measure comparable to repetition-volume in resistance training is total distance covered.

7. Which volume metric in plyometric training is equivalent to repetition-volume in resistance exercise?  
A. Height of plyometric box in inches  
B. Number of foot or hand contacts per session  
C. Amortization phase duration in milliseconds  
D. Rest interval length between depth jumps  

**Answer: B. Number of foot or hand contacts per session**  
**Rationale:** In plyometric exercise, volume is quantified as the total number of foot or hand contacts per training session.

8. In swimming or rowing, what volume metric corresponds to repetition-volume in resistance training?  
A. Total number of strokes completed  
B. Drag coefficient of the vessel  
C. Water temperature in degrees Celsius  
D. Maximal stroke rate per minute  

**Answer: A. Total number of strokes completed**  
**Rationale:** The total number of strokes completed in swimming or rowing is the volume metric comparable to repetition-volume in resistance training.


### Multiple-Select Questions (MSs)

9. Which of the following statements regarding volume-load calculation and mechanics are correct? (Select all that apply)  
A. Volume-load is directly proportional to concentric mechanical work.  
B. Vertical displacement distance is typically omitted in volume-load calculations because it is relatively constant for a given athlete.  
C. Volume-load divided by repetition-volume equals the average weight lifted per repetition.  
D. Repetition-volume and volume-load are identical metrics that provide identical data.  
E. If different sets use different weights, set volumes are calculated individually and summed.  

**Answer: A, B, C, E**  
**Rationale:** Volume-load is proportional to concentric work, omits vertical displacement due to constancy, equals average weight per rep when divided by rep-volume, and requires summing individually calculated set volumes. Repetition-volume (total reps) and volume-load (sets x reps x weight) are distinct metrics.

10. Which cross-disciplinary training volume metrics correctly match their respective sports? (Select all that apply)  
A. Aerobic Running: Total distance covered  
B. Plyometric Training: Number of foot or hand contacts  
C. Swimming/Rowing: Total number of strokes  
D. Field Sports (Jumping/Throwing): Total number of jumps or throws  
E. Weightlifting: Total rest time in seconds as volume  

**Answer: A, B, C, D**  
**Rationale:** Running distance, plyometric contacts, stroke counts in aquatic/rowing sports, and jump/throw counts in field events represent cross-disciplinary volume metrics equivalent to repetition-volume in resistance training.

11. Which formulas accurately describe resistance training volume and work metrics? (Select all that apply)  
A. Volume-Load = Sets x Repetitions x Weight  
B. Average Weight per Repetition = Volume-Load / Repetition-Volume  
C. Repetition-Volume = Total Repetitions across all sets  
D. Concentric Work = Volume-Load x Vertical Displacement  
E. Volume-Load = Total Rest Time / Repetition-Volume  

**Answer: A, B, C, D**  
**Rationale:** Volume-load is sets x reps x weight, average load per rep is volume-load / rep-volume, rep-volume is total reps, and concentric work is volume-load x displacement. Rest time does not define volume-load.

12. Which statements describe the relationship between volume-load, work, and intensity? (Select all that apply)  
A. Volume-load reflects total mechanical work and metabolic energy expenditure.  
B. Average load per rep serves as a quantitative proxy for training intensity.  
C. Calculating concentric work requires multiplying volume-load by vertical displacement.  
D. Total metabolic cost in running (Pace x Distance) is comparable to volume-load in resistance training.  
E. Volume-load eliminates the need to track load intensity or sets.  

**Answer: A, B, C, D**  
**Rationale:** Volume-load reflects work/metabolic demand, average load per rep acts as an intensity proxy, displacement converts volume-load to concentric work, and running metabolic cost (Pace x Distance) parallels volume-load. Volume-load complements rather than eliminates tracking sets and load intensity.

### Applied Short-Answer Questions

20. Distinguish between repetition-volume and volume-load, and explain how average weight per repetition is calculated.  
**Model Answer:** Repetition-volume is the total count of repetitions completed across a workout session regardless of resistance load. Volume-load quantifies total mechanical workload by multiplying sets by repetitions per set and by the weight lifted per repetition (Sets x Reps x Weight). Dividing total volume-load by repetition-volume yields the average weight lifted per repetition, which serves as a practical quantitative proxy for training intensity and work quality.

21. Explain how volume-load in resistance training compares to volume and work metrics in running, plyometrics, and sport skill practice.  
**Model Answer:** In resistance training, volume-load (Sets x Reps x Weight) reflects total mechanical work and metabolic energy cost. This parallels other athletic disciplines: in aerobic running, distance represents repetition-volume while total metabolic cost (Pace x Distance) parallels volume-load; in plyometrics, volume is measured by total foot or hand contacts; in swimming or rowing, volume is quantified by total stroke counts; and in field sports, volume is measured by the total number of jumps, throws, or pitches executed.


## Part 2: Single vs. Multiple Sets and Athlete Training Status

### Multiple-Choice Questions (MCQs)

1. For which athlete population is single-set resistance training (e.g., 1 set of 8 to 12 reps to failure) considered appropriate for generating initial strength gains?  
A. Advanced powerlifters preparing for competition  
B. Untrained individuals during their first several months of training  
C. Elite collegiate athletes during the off-season  
D. Highly trained sprinters optimizing rate of force development  

**Answer: B. Untrained individuals during their first several months of training**  
**Rationale:** Single-set training may be appropriate for untrained individuals or during the first several months of training as initial neural adaptations occur.

2. What occurs to the musculoskeletal system over time if an athlete continues to perform only one set to failure?  
A. Infinite linear strength increases without plateauing  
B. Spontaneous conversion of Type I fibers to Type IIx fibers  
C. Adaptation to the single-set stimulus, requiring multiple sets for continued strength gains  
D. Complete suppression of systemic blood lactate accumulation  

**Answer: C. Adaptation to the single-set stimulus, requiring multiple sets for continued strength gains**  
**Rationale:** The musculoskeletal system eventually adapts to the single-set stimulus to failure, requiring the added stimulus of multiple sets to promote further strength gains.

3. What did research compare regarding 3 sets of 10 repetitions without going to failure versus 1 set of 8 to 12 repetitions to failure?  
A. 1 set to failure produced double the strength gains of 3 non-failure sets  
B. 3 sets of 10 without failure enhanced strength better than 1 set of 8 to 12 to failure  
C. Both protocols produced identical cellular adaptations with zero volume differences  
D. 3 sets without failure caused severe overtraining within one week  

**Answer: B. 3 sets of 10 without failure enhanced strength better than 1 set of 8 to 12 to failure**  
**Rationale:** Performing 3 sets of 10 repetitions without going to failure enhances strength significantly better than 1 set of 8 to 12 repetitions to failure, primarily due to higher accumulated volume-load.

4. How does starting a resistance training program with multiple sets affect an athlete's rate of strength gains compared to single-set training?  
A. Multiple-set training increases strength faster than single-set training  
B. Single-set training increases strength twice as fast as multiple-set training  
C. Both approaches yield identical rates of strength adaptation  
D. Multiple-set training prevents any strength adaptation from occurring  

**Answer: A. Multiple-set training increases strength faster than single-set training**  
**Rationale:** Research shows that an athlete who performs multiple sets from the initiation of a resistance training program increases muscular strength faster than with single-set training.

5. Why is it unrealistic to expect an athlete to complete multiple sets with full RM loads at fixed repetition schemes for every exercise?  
A. RM loads automatically increase during later sets  
B. Accumulating muscular fatigue decreases the number of repetitions achievable in later sets  
C. Neural drive increases exponentially across consecutive sets  
D. Fiber type recruitment reverses order during multiple sets  

**Answer: B. Accumulating muscular fatigue decreases the number of repetitions achievable in later sets**  
**Rationale:** Accumulating metabolic and neuromuscular fatigue in later sets impairs force output, reducing the number of repetitions an athlete can perform at a fixed RM load.

6. What set volume is recommended for a beginner (untrained) athlete during initial training?  
A. 5 to 6 sets per exercise  
B. 1 to 2 sets per exercise  
C. 8 to 10 sets per exercise  
D. Zero sets per exercise  

**Answer: B. 1 to 2 sets per exercise**  
**Rationale:** It is appropriate for an athlete to perform only 1 or 2 sets as a beginner and gradually add sets as they become better trained.

7. How should training volume progress as an athlete adapts to a consistent, well-designed program?  
A. Set volume should be reduced to zero to prevent adaptation  
B. Sets should be gradually added to match guidelines for their specific training goal  
C. Repetition volume should be capped at 1 rep total per workout  
D. Load intensity should be permanently reduced to 10% of 1RM  

**Answer: B. Sets should be gradually added to match guidelines for their specific training goal**  
**Rationale:** As an athlete adapts to training, sets should be gradually added to match the specific guidelines associated with their primary training goal (e.g., strength, hypertrophy, power).

8. What primary factor contributes to the superior strength outcomes of multiple sets over single sets?  
A. Shorter rest periods between exercises  
B. Higher total accumulated volume-load  
C. Complete elimination of eccentric contractions  
D. Reduced neural motor unit recruitment  

**Answer: B. Higher total accumulated volume-load**  
**Rationale:** The higher training volume (volume-load) accumulated across multiple sets is the primary driver of superior strength and hypertrophic adaptations.


### Multiple-Select Questions (MSs)

10. Which statements accurately describe volume recommendations based on athlete training status? (Select all that apply)

11. Why do fatigue kinetics affect multiple-set performance with fixed RM loads? (Select all that apply)

12. What physiological outcomes result from multiple-set training protocols? (Select all that apply)

**Rationale:** Multiple sets drive faster strength gains, greater volume-load, enhanced protein synthesis/hypertrophy, and superior long-term strength maintenance without causing immediate atrophy.

### Applied Short-Answer Questions

20. Compare the adaptive efficacy of single-set versus multiple-set protocols for untrained versus advanced athletes.  
**Model Answer:** For untrained beginners, single-set protocols (1 set of 8–12 reps to failure) can induce initial strength gains during the first several months due to rapid neural adaptation. However, the musculoskeletal system quickly adapts to a single-set stimulus. Intermediate and advanced athletes require multiple-set protocols (3–6 sets) to accumulate higher volume-load, which is necessary to stimulate continuous muscular strength and hypertrophic adaptations over time.

21. Explain why performing 3 sets of 10 repetitions without going to failure yields superior strength gains compared to 1 set of 8 to 12 repetitions to failure.  
**Model Answer:** Performing 3 sets of 10 repetitions without going to failure yields greater total volume-load (Sets x Reps x Weight) than a single set of 8 to 12 repetitions to failure. The higher accumulated mechanical work and metabolic stress across multiple sets stimulate greater myofibrillar protein synthesis and motor unit adaptation, while avoiding extreme intra-set neuromuscular exhaustion that occurs when lifting to failure.


## Part 3: Assigning Volume by Primary Training Goal

**Answer: B. Sets of 10 repetitions**  
**Rationale:** DeLorme and Watkins originally recommended sets of 10 repetitions as ideal to increase muscular strength.

**Answer: B. 3 sets of 6 repetitions**

### Applied Short-Answer Questions

20. Summarize the volume guidelines (repetition and set ranges) for Strength, Power, Hypertrophy, and Muscular Endurance according to Table 17.11.  
**Model Answer:** According to Table 17.11:  
- **Strength:** <= 6 repetitions, 2 to 6 sets for core exercises (1 to 3 sets for assistance exercises).  
- **Single-Effort Power:** 1 to 2 repetitions, 3 to 5 sets.  
- **Multiple-Effort Power:** 3 to 5 repetitions, 3 to 5 sets.

21. Explain why power training volume is lower than strength training volume, and describe how performing multiple exercises per muscle group enhances hypertrophy.

For hypertrophy, performing 3 or more distinct exercises per muscle group targets muscle fibers from varied angles, maximizing overall volume-load, sarcoplasmic/myofibrillar stress, and motor unit recruitment.
