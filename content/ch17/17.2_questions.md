## Part 1: Exercise Types: Core, Assistance, Structural, and Power Exercises (p1)

### Multiple Choice Questions (mcq)

[mcq]

Which of the following best defines a core exercise in resistance training program design?

A. An exercise recruiting small muscle areas involving a single primary joint  
**B. An exercise recruiting one or more large muscle areas and involving two or more primary joints**  
C. Any exercise designed exclusively to isolate the abdominal musculature  
D. A single-joint exercise that requires low balance and motor coordination  

**Rationale:** According to Section 17.2, core exercises recruit one or more large muscle areas (chest, shoulder, back, hip, or thigh), involve two or more primary joints (multijoint exercises), and receive priority in program design due to their direct application to sport performance. Single-joint exercises involving smaller muscle areas are classified as assistance exercises.

[mcq]

A. Each individual articulation of the shoulder and each vertebral segment are counted as separate primary joints  
**B. All articulations of the shoulder are considered one primary joint, and the spinal column is considered one primary joint**  
C. The shoulder girdle is considered a core joint, while the glenohumeral joint is considered an assistance joint  
D. The spinal column is excluded from joint count calculations because it contains no synovial joints  

**Rationale:** Textbook Section 17.2 explicitly states that all joints at the shoulder—the glenohumeral and shoulder girdle articulations—are considered one primary joint when categorizing exercises. Similarly, the spinal column is considered a single primary joint (as demonstrated in abdominal crunches and back extensions).

[mcq]

A. Maximize power output and peak rate of force development  
**B. Prevent injury and rehabilitate specific muscle groups predisposed to repetitive stress**  
C. Serve as the foundation for multi-joint structural max-strength loading  
D. Replace all free weight multi-joint lifts for advanced elite athletes  

**Rationale:** Assistance exercises recruit smaller muscle areas across a single primary joint and isolate specific muscles. Section 17.2 notes that a common application is injury prevention (prehabilitation) and post-injury rehabilitation, such as conditioning shoulder external rotators for overhand pitchers or reconditioning quadriceps after a contusion.

[mcq]

What characteristic distinguishes a structural exercise from a general core exercise?

C. A structural exercise must be performed at slow eccentric velocities using selectorized resistance machines  
D. A structural exercise is restricted exclusively to bodyweight movement patterns  

**Rationale:** A core exercise that emphasizes loading the spine directly (e.g., back squat) or indirectly (e.g., power clean) is classified as a structural exercise. Structural exercises require active muscular stabilization of posture (e.g., maintaining a rigid torso and neutral spine) during execution.

---

[mcq]

What defines a power exercise within resistance training classification?

A. Any assistance exercise performed with maximal weight loads for 1 rep  
**B. A structural exercise that is performed very quickly or explosively**  
C. A single-joint machine exercise performed to muscular failure  
D. An isometric abdominal hold sustained for longer than 60 seconds  

**Rationale:** Section 17.2 defines a power exercise as a structural exercise that is performed very quickly or explosively. Power exercises are assigned to athletes when explosive movement and high rates of force development are sport-specific training priorities.

**Rationale:** The back squat recruits large muscle groups (quadriceps, gluteals, hamstrings) across two primary joints (hip and knee), meeting the exact textbook criteria for a multi-joint core exercise. Biceps curls, calf raises, and abdominal crunches involve single primary joints and/or smaller muscle groups, classifying them as assistance exercises.

Section 17.2 states that power exercises (e.g., power clean, snatch) apply a fast rate of force development (RFD) and high power, making them highly relevant for sports requiring explosive jumping, sprinting, or throwing.

### Applied Short Answer Questions (sa)

[sa]

**Clinical/Athletic Prompt:** A strength and conditioning coach is designing an off-season program for a collegiate volleyball player. Compare and contrast the roles of core structural lifts (e.g., back squat) and single-joint assistance exercises (e.g., rotator cuff cuff external rotations) in this athlete's routine.

**Model Answer:** Core structural exercises like the back squat recruit large muscle groups across multiple primary joints while directly loading the spine, requiring active postural stabilization of a rigid torso. These lifts receive top priority because they develop foundational lower-body strength and multi-joint kinetic chain transfer for vertical jumping. In contrast, single-joint assistance exercises like rotator cuff external rotations recruit smaller muscle areas across one primary joint (shoulder complex). Their primary role is injury prevention (prehabilitation) and muscular balance, isolating the external rotators to withstand repetitive high-velocity spiking and serving demands without attempting to drive primary power adaptations.

**Evaluation Criteria:**
- Differentiates core structural lifts (multi-joint, large muscle mass, spinal loading) from assistance lifts (single-joint, smaller muscle groups).
- Explains the performance priority of core structural lifts for developing vertical jump strength and kinetic chain stability.
- Identifies the prehabilitation and injury prevention role of rotator cuff assistance exercises in volleyball athletes.

---

[sa]

**Clinical/Athletic Prompt:** Explain the physiological and biomechanical rationale for incorporating power exercises (e.g., power clean) versus heavy non-explosive structural exercises (e.g., heavy back squat) for a track and field high jumper.

**Model Answer:** Heavy non-explosive structural exercises like the back squat load the spine directly and build maximal force production capacity by recruiting high-threshold motor units under high loads at lower velocities. However, high jumping requires generating force in fractions of a second upon plant and takeoff. Power exercises like the power clean are structural lifts performed explosively at high movement velocities, specifically targeting a fast rate of force development (RFD) and maximal power output ($P = \text{Force} \times \text{Velocity}$). While the back squat establishes maximal strength potential, the power clean trains the neuromuscular system to translate that force into high-velocity explosive power matching the sport-specific takeoff mechanics of the high jump.

**Evaluation Criteria:**
- Defines structural exercises and their role in loading the spine and building maximal strength.
- Defines power exercises as explosive structural lifts that maximize rate of force development (RFD).
- Connects high movement velocity and RFD to the sport-specific takeoff demands of high jumping.

## Part 2: Movement Analysis of the Sport: Sport-Specificity and Muscle Balance (p2)

### Multiple Choice Questions (mcq)

[mcq]

Which principle dictates that resistance training exercises should match the body and limb movement patterns, joint ranges of motion, and muscle recruitment of the target sport?

A. Overload principle  
**B. Specific Adaptation to Imposed Demands (SAID) principle**  
C. Reversibility principle  
D. Diminishing returns principle  

**Rationale:** The SAID principle (also known as the specificity concept) dictates that the closer a training activity mimics the actual sport movement in pattern, joint range of motion, and muscular involvement, the greater the positive transfer to sport performance.

**Rationale:** Section 17.2 explicitly notes that while both the back squat and leg press strengthen the primary hip and knee extensors used in jumping, the back squat is performed from an erect body position with balance and weight-bearing forces, making it more relevant to jumping mechanics than the seated machine leg press.
