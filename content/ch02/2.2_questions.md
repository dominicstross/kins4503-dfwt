2. Which anatomical plane divides the human body into left and right sections?  
A. Frontal plane  
B. Sagittal plane  
C. Transverse plane  
D. Horizontal plane  

**Answer: B. Sagittal plane**  
**Rationale:** The sagittal plane slices the body into left and right sections, not necessarily at the anatomical midpoint.

### Applied Short-Answer Questions

20. Describe the posture and orientation of the human body in the standard anatomical position, and explain why this standardized baseline is essential in kinesiology.  
**Model Answer:** In the standard anatomical position, the individual stands erect with the head facing forward, arms positioned straight down at the sides, and palms facing forward (fully supinated), with the lower limbs parallel and feet pointing forward. This standardized posture is essential in biomechanics and kinesiology because it establishes a universal, unambiguous spatial baseline. Without a standardized reference point, directional terms (such as anterior, posterior, medial, and lateral) and descriptions of joint articulations would become contradictory and dependent on arbitrary subject positioning.

21. Define the three cardinal anatomical planes of the human body, identifying the spatial sections created by each and providing the textbook exercise example that corresponds to each plane.  
**Model Answer:** The three cardinal anatomical planes are: (1) The Sagittal Plane, which slices the body vertically into left and right sections (not necessarily at the midpoint) and governs forward-backward flexion and extension movements; the text cites the standing barbell curl as a sagittal exercise. (2) The Frontal (Coronal) Plane, which slices the body vertically into front and back (anterior and posterior) sections and governs lateral side-to-side movements such as abduction and adduction; the text cites the standing lateral dumbbell raise as a frontal exercise. (3) The Transverse (Horizontal) Plane, which slices the body horizontally into upper and lower (superior and inferior) sections and governs rotational and horizontal limb motions; the text cites the dumbbell fly as a transverse exercise.  
**Evaluation Criteria:**  
- Define the sagittal plane (left-right division) and associate it with the standing barbell curl.  
- Define the frontal plane (front-back division) and associate it with the standing lateral dumbbell raise.  
- Define the transverse plane (upper-lower division) and associate it with the dumbbell fly.  
- Note that planar slices do not necessarily occur at the anatomical midpoint.
