1. Prompt: Explain the progression along the training continuum from functional overreaching (FOR) to nonfunctional overreaching (NFOR) and overtraining syndrome (OTS), detailing recovery timeframes for each.  
Model Answer: The training continuum begins with planned acute overload and functional overreaching (FOR), where heavy training temporarily depresses performance, but recovery and supercompensation occur within days to weeks following a taper. If excessive training continues without adequate rest, the athlete enters nonfunctional overreaching (NFOR), leading to performance stagnation or decrements lasting weeks to months alongside fatigue, mood alterations, and hormonal shifts. If unmanaged, NFOR evolves into overtraining syndrome (OTS), a severe clinical state featuring prolonged neuroendocrine and biological maladaptation that requires six months or longer for full recovery and can jeopardize an athlete's career.  
Grading Criteria:  
- Define FOR as planned short-term performance depression that recovers in days to weeks with supercompensation.  
- Define NFOR as prolonged performance stagnation/decrease lasting weeks to months due to inadequate recovery.  
- Define OTS as severe neuroendocrine maladaptation requiring 6 months or longer for restoration.

2. Prompt: Contrast sympathetic overtraining syndrome with parasympathetic overtraining syndrome in terms of autonomic activity, symptoms, and target athlete populations.  
Model Answer: Sympathetic overtraining syndrome is characterized by elevated resting sympathetic nervous system activity (increased resting heart rate, restlessness, insomnia, and excitability) and develops early in the overtraining continuum, predominantly affecting younger athletes involved in high-velocity speed, power, and strength sports. In contrast, parasympathetic overtraining syndrome involves increased resting and exercise parasympathetic activity (low resting heart rate, lethargy, decreased blood pressure, and suppressed endocrine drive), representing end-stage physiological exhaustion that predominantly affects chronic aerobic endurance athletes.  
Grading Criteria:  
- Identify sympathetic OTS as elevated resting sympathetic drive in young speed/power athletes.  
- Identify parasympathetic OTS as end-stage vagal dominance and endocrine suppression in endurance athletes.  
- Compare resting symptom presentations (high HR/restlessness vs low HR/lethargy).
