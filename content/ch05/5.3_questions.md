### Applied Short Answer Questions (sa)

[sa]

**Clinical/Athletic Prompt:** Explain why a strength coach must incorporate progressive overload in a resistance program to continuously stimulate bone mineral accretion over a multi-month training block.

**Model Answer:** When mechanical loads exceed the Minimal Essential Strain (MES) threshold, osteoblasts deposit new bone matrix along the periosteum. This increases outer bone diameter, which distributes applied mechanical forces over a larger surface area. As a result, stress per unit area decreases, and the same absolute training load that initially exceeded MES will eventually fall below the threshold. Progressive overload (increasing resistance) is mandatory to re-exceed the elevated MES threshold and maintain an ongoing osteogenic stimulus.

**Evaluation Criteria:**
- State that exceeding MES triggers osteoblast activity and periosteal bone deposition.
- Explain that increased bone diameter distributes forces over a larger surface area, lowering stress per unit area.
- Conclude that progressive overload is necessary to re-exceed the higher MES threshold as bone adapts.

---

[sa]

**Clinical/Athletic Prompt:** Contrast the structural characteristics and adaptive response rates of cortical bone versus trabecular bone under mechanical loading.

**Model Answer:** Cortical (compact) bone forms the dense, rigid outer protective shell of skeletal structures; it has a low surface area-to-mass ratio and adapts at a relatively slow rate. In contrast, trabecular (spongy) bone is composed of porous, interlocking plates surrounding marrow cavities; it possesses a high surface area-to-mass ratio and a high metabolic turnover rate, allowing it to adapt significantly more rapidly to mechanical strain than cortical bone.

**Evaluation Criteria:**
- Describe cortical bone as dense, compact outer shell with low surface area-to-mass ratio and slow adaptive rate.
- Describe trabecular bone as porous interlocking plates with high surface area-to-mass ratio and rapid adaptive rate.
- Identify trabecular bone as the more metabolically active and responsive tissue during early mechanical loading.

**Clinical/Athletic Prompt:** An athlete recovering from a patellar tendon strain asks why tendon rehab takes many months compared to a muscle strain. Provide a physiological explanation based on tissue vascularity and cellular metabolism.

Consequently, collagen synthesis, fibril hypertrophy, and covalent cross-linking proceed at a much slower rate, resulting in prolonged healing timelines for tendons compared to muscle tissue.

### Applied Short Answer Questions (sa)

[sa]

**Clinical/Athletic Prompt:** A physical therapy patient asks why 6 weeks of knee joint immobilization in a rigid cast caused thinning of their knee articular cartilage. Explain the physiological mechanism behind immobilization-induced cartilage atrophy.

**Model Answer:** Articular hyaline cartilage is completely avascular and lacks capillary blood vessels to deliver oxygen and nutrients. Resident cartilage cells (chondrocytes) depend entirely on the passive diffusion of nutrients from surrounding synovial fluid. Under normal conditions, joint movement creates cyclic pressure differentials that pump synovial fluid into the cartilage matrix. Prolonged immobilization eliminates these pressure gradients, halting fluid movement and causing chondrocyte starvation, matrix resorption, and articular cartilage atrophy (thinning).

**Evaluation Criteria:**
- State that articular cartilage is completely avascular and lacks blood vessels.
- Explain that chondrocytes rely on passive diffusion of nutrients from synovial fluid driven by joint movement.
- Explain that immobilization halts joint pressure changes, causing chondrocyte starvation, matrix resorption, and cartilage thinning.
