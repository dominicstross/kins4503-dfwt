## Part 1: Acute Anabolic and Catabolic Hormonal Responses (p1)

Which acute anabolic and catabolic hormones undergo rapid elevation for up to 30 minutes following an acute resistance training session in men?

**B. Testosterone, growth hormone (GH) molecular variants, and cortisol**

D. Estrogen, progesterone, and luteinizing hormone  

**Rationale:** Heavy anaerobic exercise stimulates rapid, transient increases in circulating testosterone, growth hormone (GH) molecular variants, and cortisol. These levels peak immediately post-exercise and return toward baseline within 30 minutes.

C. Static passive stretching, low repetition volume, and 5-minute rest intervals  
D. Submaximal steady-state walking at 30% VO2max with 3-minute rest periods  

**Rationale:** Large muscle mass exercises (e.g., squats, deadlifts, cleans) combined with moderate-to-high loads (70–85% 1RM), high set volumes, and short rest periods (30–90 seconds) maximize metabolic stress and muscle recruitment, driving peak acute surges in anabolic and catabolic hormones.

**B. Hydrogen ion (H+) accumulation and elevated blood lactate levels**

**Rationale:** Intramuscular hydrogen ion (H+) accumulation and high blood lactate concentrations serve as primary metabolic triggers that stimulate acute surges in growth hormone and cortisol secretion.

**Rationale:** Systemic IGF-I is synthesized primarily in the liver in response to growth hormone secretion and exhibits a delayed response. ### Applied Short Answer Questions (SA)

[sa]

**Clinical/Athletic Prompt:** Compare the acute endocrine responses produced by a heavy powerlifting protocol (5 sets of 2 reps at 95% 1RM with 5-minute rest) versus a hypertrophy protocol (4 sets of 10 reps at 75% 1RM with 60-second rest).

**Model Answer:** 
1. Powerlifting Protocol (95% 1RM, 5-min rest): Produces high neural drive and mechanical stress but minimal metabolic byproduct accumulation. Consequently, acute growth hormone and cortisol surges are modest, although testosterone release is stimulated by high motor unit recruitment.
2. Hypertrophy Protocol (75% 1RM, 60-s rest): Involves high total work volume and high metabolic stress, causing rapid accumulation of hydrogen ions and blood lactate. This environment drives significantly larger acute spikes in growth hormone, cortisol, and testosterone immediately post-workout.

**Evaluation Criteria:**
- Contrast the neural focus of heavy powerlifting with the metabolic focus of hypertrophy training.
- State that short rest periods (60 s) and moderate loads (75% 1RM) elicit greater acute GH and cortisol spikes.
- Identify blood lactate/H+ accumulation as the primary driver of augmented growth hormone release.

---

[sa]

**Clinical/Athletic Prompt:** Explain the physiological rationale for consuming a carbohydrate-protein supplement immediately following an intense resistance training workout regarding insulin and protein kinetics.

**Model Answer:** Resistance exercise alone does not stimulate acute insulin release. Consuming carbohydrates post-workout raises blood glucose, triggering pancreatic insulin secretion. Concurrently, ingested protein supplies essential amino acids. Elevated insulin acts as a potent anti-catabolic signal that suppresses muscle protein breakdown, while high amino acid availability drives Akt/mTOR signaling for muscle protein synthesis, transforming net protein balance from negative to positive.

**Evaluation Criteria:**
- State that exercise alone does not stimulate insulin and that nutrition is required.
- Explain that carbohydrate intake triggers insulin release to suppress protein breakdown.
- Explain that protein intake provides amino acids to drive muscle protein synthesis via Akt/mTOR.

**Clinical/Athletic Prompt:** Explain why basal resting testosterone levels do not continuously rise over years of heavy strength training, and describe the physiological benefit of maintaining stable resting levels.

**Model Answer:** Basal resting testosterone levels remain relatively stable because permanent chronic elevation would cause continuous receptor binding, leading to target tissue androgen receptor downregulation and desensitization (similar to insulin resistance). [sa]

**Clinical/Athletic Prompt:** Discuss how monitoring the resting cortisol-to-testosterone ratio can assist strength and conditioning coaches in identifying overtraining in elite athletes.

**Model Answer:** Testosterone is a primary anabolic hormone promoting muscle protein synthesis, whereas cortisol is a primary catabolic hormone driving protein breakdown under stress. A healthy, adapting athlete exhibits a high anabolic-to-catabolic ratio. If an athlete experiences excessive volume/intensity without adequate recovery, resting testosterone drops while resting cortisol remains chronically elevated. This reduction in the testosterone-to-cortisol ratio signals systemic overtraining, impaired tissue repair, and heightened catabolism, prompting coaches to reduce training load.

**Evaluation Criteria:**
- Define testosterone as anabolic (synthesis) and cortisol as catabolic (breakdown).
- Explain that a declining ratio indicates high catabolic stress and incomplete recovery.
- Identify the clinical outcome as overtraining syndrome requiring training load reduction.

### Applied Short Answer Questions (SA)

[sa]

**Clinical/Athletic Prompt:** Describe the biphasic time course of Androgen Receptor (AR) content following high-volume resistance training (Ratamess et al.), and explain how peri-workout nutrition modifies this response (Kraemer et al.).

**Model Answer:** 
1. Biphasic Time Course (Ratamess et al.): High-volume resistance exercise (e.g., 6 sets of 10 reps) causes an initial acute downregulation (reduction) of AR content at 1 hour post-exercise. Over the subsequent recovery window, AR expression undergoes compensatory upregulation, peaking between 48 and 72 hours post-workout.
2. Nutritional Modification (Kraemer et al.): Ingesting a protein-carbohydrate supplement immediately before and after exercise attenuates or prevents the initial 1-hour post-workout AR downregulation. Preserving AR availability early post-exercise optimizes testosterone binding and accelerates anabolic recovery.

**Evaluation Criteria:**
- Describe the biphasic kinetics: initial 1-hour AR downregulation followed by 48–72h upregulation.
- Cite Ratamess et al. regarding volume-dependent 1-hour AR downregulation.
- Cite Kraemer et al. showing that peri-workout protein-carbohydrate intake attenuates the initial 1-hour AR drop.

**Clinical/Athletic Prompt:** Explain the molecular mechanism by which free testosterone interacts with Androgen Receptors (AR) to stimulate skeletal muscle hypertrophy in Type II fibers.

**Model Answer:** Free testosterone diffuses across the sarcolemma into the cytoplasm of Type II muscle fibers, which possess a high baseline AR density. Testosterone binds to the intracellular AR, forming a hormone-receptor complex. This complex translocates into the cell nucleus and binds to specific DNA regulatory elements. This binding activates RNA polymerase to stimulate mRNA transcription for contractile proteins (actin and myosin). The exported mRNA is translated at the ribosome (amplified by Akt/mTOR signaling) to assemble new myofilaments, expanding muscle fiber cross-sectional area.

- Identify free testosterone diffusing into Type II fibers to bind intracellular ARs.
- Describe the translocation of the hormone-receptor complex into the nucleus to bind DNA.
- State that this binding stimulates mRNA transcription for actin and myosin, driving hypertrophy.
