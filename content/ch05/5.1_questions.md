Where in the central nervous system does increased motor unit activation originate when an athlete intends to exert maximal muscular force?  

**A. Higher brain centers and the primary motor cortex**

## Part 2: Adaptations of Motor Units (p2)

### Multiple Choice Questions (mcq)

What is the functional unit of the neuromuscular system?  

**A. The motor unit, consisting of an alpha motor neuron and all the muscle fibers it innervates**  
B. The muscle spindle, consisting of intrafusal fibers and gamma motor neurons  
C. The sarcomere, consisting of actin and myosin myofilaments  
D. The neuromuscular junction, consisting of acetylcholine and the synaptic cleft  

**Rationale:** The functional unit of the neuromuscular system is the motor unit, which comprises a single alpha motor neuron and all the skeletal muscle fibers innervated by its axon terminals.

---

[mcq]

How does the motor unit innervation ratio differ between small, intricate muscles and large, powerful limb muscles?  

**A. Small intricate muscles innervate <10 fibers per motor neuron, whereas large powerful muscles innervate >100 fibers**  
B. Small intricate muscles innervate >1000 fibers per motor neuron, whereas large powerful muscles innervate 2 fibers  
C. Both small and large muscles innervate exactly 50 fibers per motor neuron  
D. Large powerful muscles contain no alpha motor neurons  

**Rationale:** Innervation ratios adapt to functional demands: small, intricate muscles (such as extraocular or hand muscles) innervate <10 fibers per motor neuron for fine control, whereas large trunk and limb muscles innervate >100 fibers for high force.

---

[mcq]

What phenomenon produces augmented muscle force when motor unit firing rates increase so rapidly that action potentials overlap before the muscle fiber can relax?  

**A. Summation**  
B. Tetanic decay  
C. Selective decruitment  
D. Bilateral deficit  

**Rationale:** Summation occurs when elevated motor unit firing rates cause successive action potentials to arrive before the muscle fiber completely relaxes from a previous twitch, resulting in cumulative, augmented force development.

---

[mcq]

According to Henneman's size principle, in what order are motor units recruited as muscular force demands increase?  

**A. In ascending order based on recruitment threshold, starting with low-threshold Type I units up to high-threshold Type II units**  
B. In descending order, starting with high-threshold Type IIx units down to Type I units  
C. In a random asynchronous pattern regardless of force demands  
D. Exclusively in alphabetical order based on anatomical muscle names  

**Rationale:** Henneman's size principle dictates that motor units are recruited in ascending order according to their recruitment thresholds and twitch forces—activating low-threshold Type I slow-twitch units first, followed by Type IIa and Type IIx fast-twitch units as force requirements rise.

---

[mcq]

Why does heavy resistance training (>85% 1RM) cause hypertrophy across all skeletal muscle fiber types?  

**A. Because high force demands require the sequential recruitment of the entire motor unit pool from low- to high-threshold**  
B. Because heavy loading selectively destroys slow-twitch muscle fibers  
C. Because heavy weights inhibit high-threshold fast-twitch motor units  
D. Because heavy resistance training relies solely on the aerobic oxidative energy system  

**Rationale:** Heavy resistance training (>85% 1RM) forces the central nervous system to recruit the entire motor unit pool sequentially (from low to high threshold) per the size principle, exposing all muscle fiber types to mechanical tension and driving hypertrophy across all fiber types.

---

[mcq]

What exception to Henneman's size principle allows athletes to inhibit lower-threshold motor units and directly activate high-threshold units during explosive actions?  

**A. Selective recruitment**  
B. Summation failure  
C. Bilateral deficit  
D. Cross-education  

**Rationale:** Selective recruitment is an exception to the size principle wherein the central nervous system selectively inhibits lower-threshold slow-twitch motor units and preferentially activates higher-threshold fast-twitch motor units during rapid, high-velocity, or ballistic movements.

**Rationale:** Explosive jumps feature movement durations of <0.4 seconds. ### Applied Short Answer Questions (sa)

[sa]

**Clinical/Athletic Prompt:** Compare Henneman's size principle with selective recruitment, explaining the specific athletic tasks and movement durations under which each strategy operates.  

**Model Answer:** Henneman's size principle states that motor units are recruited in ascending order based on threshold and firing rate—activating low-threshold Type I slow-twitch fibers first, followed sequentially by Type IIa and Type IIx fast-twitch fibers as force demands increase. This orderly recruitment operates during heavy, controlled resistance exercises (e.g., a heavy 3RM back squat >85% 1RM).
Selective recruitment is an exception to the size principle occurring during high-velocity, ballistic movements (e.g., depth jumps, Olympic cleans, sprinting). Under explosive conditions where movement duration is <0.4 seconds, there is insufficient time for sequential recruitment. The central nervous system inhibits low-threshold slow-twitch units and preferentially activates high-threshold fast-twitch motor units directly to maximize rate of force development.  

**Evaluation Criteria:**
- Define Henneman's size principle as orderly ascending recruitment (low to high threshold) during heavy controlled loading.
- Define selective recruitment as inhibiting low-threshold units to activate high-threshold fast-twitch units directly.
- Identify brief movement duration (<0.4 s) in ballistic tasks (jumps, sprints) as the trigger for selective recruitment.

---

[sa]

**Clinical/Athletic Prompt:** Explain the mechanism of motor unit summation, and describe why progressive overload is physiologically necessary as muscle hypertrophy occurs.  

**Model Answer:** Summation occurs when elevated motor unit discharge firing rates cause action potentials to arrive at the neuromuscular junction in rapid succession before muscle fibers can completely relax from preceding twitch contractions. These overlapping action potentials produce additive, cumulative tension, maximizing force output and rate of force development.
As an athlete undergoes muscle hypertrophy following chronic training (e.g., a 5% size increase after 9 weeks), the muscle belly becomes more mechanically efficient. Consequently, fewer muscle fibers and a lower level of neural activation are required to lift any given absolute load. Therefore, progressive overload (continually increasing external resistance) is physiologically required to maintain high motor unit recruitment thresholds and drive ongoing neuromuscular adaptations.  

**Evaluation Criteria:**
- Define summation as cumulative force augmentation from overlapping action potentials due to high firing rates.
- Explain that hypertrophied muscle requires less neural activation and fewer active fibers for a given absolute load.
- Connect this efficiency adaptation directly to the physiological requirement for progressive overload.
