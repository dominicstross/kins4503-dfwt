## Part 1: Muscular Growth (p1)

### Multiple Choice Questions (MCQ)

[mcq]

What is the primary biological mechanism responsible for skeletal muscle hypertrophy following chronic anaerobic resistance training?

A. An increase in total skeletal muscle fiber count via longitudinal fiber splitting  
B. <mark>A net positive accretion of actin and myosin myofilaments increasing myofibrillar cross-sectional area</mark>  
C. Proliferation of non-contractile connective tissue sheaths without myofibrillar expansion  
D. Selective reduction of intracellular sarcoplasmic fluid volume  

**Rationale:** Muscle hypertrophy refers to the enlargement of muscle fiber cross-sectional area (CSA), driven by a net positive balance of protein synthesis over degradation. This process involves synthesizing the primary contractile proteins actin and myosin within myofibrils and adding new myofilaments to the outer layers of existing myofibrils. Fiber splitting (hyperplasia) plays a negligible role in humans.

---

[mcq]

Which intracellular anabolic signaling cascade is directly activated by mechanical tension to initiate muscle protein synthesis and myogenesis?

A. The AMPK pathway  
B. <mark>The Akt/mTOR signaling pathway</mark>  
C. The myostatin stimulation pathway  
D. The lactate-dehydrogenase pathway  

**Rationale:** Mechanical deformation of skeletal muscle under tension activates protein kinase B (Akt) and the mammalian target of rapamycin (mTOR). The Akt/mTOR cascade directly stimulates mRNA translation to synthesize contractile proteins (actin and myosin), driving myogenesis and muscle growth. AMPK activation is associated with cellular energy depletion, and myostatin is an inhibitory factor that is down-regulated during hypertrophy.

[mcq]

How does resistance training alter intracellular expression of the regulatory protein myostatin to facilitate muscle growth?

A. It up-regulates myostatin to promote rapid fiber splitting

C. It converts myostatin into an active isoform of actin  
D. It binds myostatin to sodium-potassium ATPase pumps to enhance sarcolemmal voltage  

**Rationale:** Myostatin is a gene product that acts as a potent negative regulator (inhibitory factor) of skeletal muscle mass. Resistance training suppresses (down-regulates) myostatin expression, thereby removing molecular constraints on muscle protein synthesis and accelerating net protein accretion.

### Applied Short Answer (SA)

[sa]

**Clinical/Athletic Prompt:** A novice collegiate athlete gains 15% in maximal squat strength after 3 weeks (6 sessions) of resistance training but shows zero increase in thigh circumference. Explain the physiological mechanisms responsible for this early strength gain and outline when structural hypertrophy will become measurable.

**Model Answer:** Initial strength increases during the first 1 to 4 weeks (1 to 8 sessions) of resistance training are driven almost exclusively by central neural adaptations rather than muscle hypertrophy. These neural changes include increased agonist motor unit recruitment, elevated alpha motor neuron discharge rates (firing frequency), enhanced motor unit synchronization, and decreased Golgi tendon organ inhibition. Structural muscle fiber hypertrophy requires a net accretion of actin and myosin myofilaments over time, which requires a minimum threshold of more than 16 consistent workouts (typically 6 to 8 weeks) before measurable increases in muscle cross-sectional area (CSA) and thigh circumference manifest.

**Evaluation Criteria:**
- Identifies central neural adaptations (recruitment, firing rate, synchronization) as the primary driver of early 1–4 week strength gains.
- States that structural hypertrophy requires net contractile protein (actin/myosin) accretion via protein synthesis exceeding degradation.
- Cites the physiological requirement of >16 consistent workouts before structural cross-sectional area increases become measurable.

---

[sa]

**Clinical/Athletic Prompt:** Compare the primary acute training variables and physiological stimuli of a heavy mechanical tension protocol versus a metabolic stress protocol for muscle hypertrophy.

**Model Answer:** A heavy mechanical tension protocol utilizes heavy loads (>85% 1RM), low repetitions, and long rest intervals (2 to 5 minutes). This stimulus produces high physical strain across the sarcolemma and sarcomeres, recruiting high-threshold fast-twitch motor units and activating the Akt/mTOR signaling cascade to drive myofibrillar protein synthesis. In contrast, a metabolic stress protocol utilizes moderate loads (60% to 75% 1RM), higher volume (3–6 sets of 8–12 reps), and short rest periods (30 to 90 seconds). This stimulus causes intramuscular accumulation of glycolytic metabolites (hydrogen ions, lactate, inorganic phosphate), cell swelling, and acute surges in anabolic hormones (testosterone, growth hormone), driving sarcoplasmic and protein accretion through metabolic pathways.

**Evaluation Criteria:**
- Details mechanical tension variables (>85% 1RM, long rest) and mechanism (high physical tension, Akt/mTOR pathway activation).
- Details metabolic stress variables (60–75% 1RM, 8–12 reps, 30–90 s rest) and mechanism (metabolite accumulation, cell swelling, hormone surges).
- Explains how both pathways converge to stimulate net muscle protein accretion.

According to Henneman's size principle, what training condition is mandatory to initiate hypertrophic adaptations in high-threshold Type II muscle fibers?

### Applied Short Answer (SA)

[sa]

**Clinical/Athletic Prompt:** Explain why a competitive powerlifter exhibits lower muscle capillary and mitochondrial density per unit area than an elite bodybuilder, despite both having large skeletal muscle mass.

**Model Answer:** The difference in capillary and mitochondrial density stems from their distinct training protocols. Powerlifters utilize low-repetition, heavy-load sets (>85% 1RM) with long rest intervals (3 to 5 minutes). This heavy mechanical loading drives massive myofibrillar hypertrophy (contractile protein accretion), expanding fiber cross-sectional area (CSA) without generating sustained metabolic acidosis. Because fiber CSA expands faster than new capillaries or mitochondria are formed, powerlifters experience a pronounced *dilutional reduction* in mitochondrial and capillary density per unit volume. In contrast, bodybuilders utilize high-volume sets (8–12 reps) with short rest intervals (30 to 90 seconds). This protocol generates severe metabolic stress, high hydrogen ion ($H^+$) accumulation, and transient hypoxia, which stimulates angiogenic growth factors (such as VEGF) to form new capillaries (angiogenesis) and preserve higher capillary density despite fiber hypertrophy.

**Evaluation Criteria:**
- Explains the dilutional reduction mechanism in powerlifters (myofibrillar CSA growth outpacing organelle/capillary growth under long-rest protocols).
- Explains why bodybuilders maintain higher capillary density (high volume, short rest 30–90 s creating metabolic stress and H+ accumulation).
- Identifies metabolic stress and angiogenesis as key drivers preserving vascular density in bodybuilders.

**Clinical/Athletic Prompt:** Detail the substrate supercompensation adaptations that occur in high-energy phosphagens and muscle glycogen following chronic resistance training, citing specific quantitative changes from research.

**Model Answer:** Chronic resistance training triggers a metabolic supercompensation response in resting energy substrates following repeated training-induced depletion. For high-energy phosphagens, research by MacDougall and colleagues demonstrated that 5 months of heavy resistance training (3–5 sets of 8–10 reps) increased resting intramuscular creatine phosphate (CP) stores by 28% and resting ATP concentrations by 18%. For muscle glycogen, high-volume glycolytic resistance training protocols (bodybuilding-style training) stimulate intense glycogen breakdown during exercise, activating glycogen synthase during recovery. This results in a glycogen supercompensation effect, elevating resting muscle glycogen stores by up to 112% above pre-training baseline levels. These adaptations increase immediate energy availability and delay fatigue during high-volume anaerobic performance.
