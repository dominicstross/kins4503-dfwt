## Concept Part 1: Acute Cardiovascular Responses and Hemodynamics (p1)

### [mcq]

1. What is the mathematical relationship used to calculate cardiac output (Q)?

A. Cardiac output = End-diastolic volume \\times Heart rate  
**B. Cardiac output = Stroke volume \\times Heart rate**  
C. Cardiac output = Systolic blood pressure \\times Heart rate  
D. Cardiac output = Mean arterial pressure \\div Total peripheral resistance  

**Rationale:** Cardiac output (Q) is defined as the volume of blood pumped by the heart per minute and is calculated as the product of stroke volume (SV) and heart rate (HR), expressed as Q = SV \\times HR.

2. At approximately what percentage of maximal oxygen uptake (\\dot{V}O_2\\text{max}) does stroke volume typically plateau in untrained and moderately trained individuals?

A. 15% to 25% \\dot{V}O_2\\text{max}  
**B. 40% to 50% \\dot{V}O_2\\text{max}**  
C. 70% to 80% \\dot{V}O_2\\text{max}  
D. 90% to 100% \\dot{V}O_2\\text{max}  

**Rationale:** In untrained and moderately trained individuals, stroke volume increases progressively with exercise intensity up to approximately 40% to 50% of \\dot{V}O_2\\text{max}, after which it reaches a plateau.

3. Which physiological mechanism describes the increased force of myocardial contraction resulting from enhanced venous return stretching the cardiac muscle fibers?

A. Fick principle  
B. Bohr effect  
**C. Frank-Starling mechanism**  
D. Haldane effect  

**Rationale:** The Frank-Starling mechanism states that as end-diastolic volume increases due to elevated venous return, the myocardial fibers are stretched, resulting in a more forceful contraction and increased ejection fraction.

4. What is the estimated maximal heart rate (HR_max) for a 30-year-old athlete using the standard age-predicted formula (220 - age)?

A. 170 beats/min  
B. 180 beats/min  
**C. 190 beats/min**  
D. 200 beats/min  

**Rationale:** Using the standard age-predicted maximum heart rate equation (HR_max = 220 - age), a 30-year-old athlete's estimated HR_max is 220 - 30 = 190 beats/min (with a standard deviation of \\pm 10 to 12 beats/min).

5. According to the Fick equation, oxygen uptake (\\dot{V}O_2) is the product of cardiac output (Q) and which blood gas parameter?

A. Arterial partial pressure of oxygen (P_aO_2)  
**B. Arteriovenous oxygen difference (a-\\bar{v}O_2 difference)**  
C. Plasma bicarbonate concentration  
D. Mixed venous oxygen saturation  

**Rationale:** The Fick equation defines oxygen uptake as \\dot{V}O_2 = Q \\times a-\\bar{v}O_2 difference, where the arteriovenous oxygen difference represents the amount of oxygen extracted by active tissues from the blood.

6. During acute aerobic exercise, how do systolic blood pressure (SBP) and diastolic blood pressure (DBP) typically respond as intensity increases?

A. SBP remains unchanged while DBP increases linearly  
**B. SBP increases linearly while DBP remains stable or slightly decreases**  
C. Both SBP and DBP increase dramatically in parallel  
D. Both SBP and DBP drop significantly due to systemic vasodilation  

**Rationale:** During acute aerobic exercise, systolic blood pressure increases linearly with exercise workload (from resting 110-139 mmHg up to 220-260 mmHg at peak), whereas diastolic blood pressure remains stable or decreases slightly (60-89 mmHg) due to decreased peripheral vascular resistance in active skeletal muscle.

7. What metric is calculated by multiplying heart rate by systolic blood pressure to estimate myocardial oxygen consumption and cardiac workload?

A. Mean arterial pressure (MAP)  
B. Pulse pressure  
**C. Rate-pressure product (RPP)**  
D. Ejection fraction  

**Rationale:** Rate-pressure product (RPP = HR \\times SBP), also known as double product, provides a direct non-invasive estimate of myocardial work and oxygen demand.

8. During maximal aerobic exercise, approximately what percentage of total cardiac output is redirected to active skeletal muscle?

A. 15% to 20%  
B. 40% to 50%  
C. 65% to 75%  
**D. 85% to 90%**  

**Rationale:** At rest, skeletal muscle receives only 15% to 20% of cardiac output. During maximal aerobic exertion, selective vasoconstriction in non-active visceral organs combined with vasodilation in active muscles redirects up to 85% to 90% of cardiac output to skeletal muscle.

## Concept Part 2: Respiratory Mechanics and Pulmonary Gas Exchange (p2)

### [mcq]

1. What formula defines minute ventilation (\\dot{V}_E), the total volume of air exhaled per minute?

A. Minute ventilation = Tidal volume \\div Breathing frequency  
**B. Minute ventilation = Tidal volume \\times Breathing frequency**  
C. Minute ventilation = Vital capacity \\times Heart rate  
D. Minute ventilation = Alveolar ventilation \\times Dead space volume  

**Rationale:** Minute ventilation (\\dot{V}_E) is calculated as the product of tidal volume (TV, volume of air per breath) and breathing frequency (f_b, breaths per minute), expressed as \\dot{V}_E = TV \\times f_b.

2. What is the typical resting minute ventilation (\\dot{V}_E) in a healthy adult, and to what range can it surge during maximal aerobic exertion?

A. Resting 2 L/min; Maximal 30 to 45 L/min  
**B. Resting 10 to 15 L/min; Maximal 90 to 150 L/min**  
C. Resting 25 L/min; Maximal 200 to 250 L/min  
D. Resting 50 L/min; Maximal 300 to 400 L/min  

**Rationale:** Resting minute ventilation averages 10 to 15 L/min (TV \\approx 0.5 L, f_b \\approx 12-15 breaths/min). During maximal exercise, \\dot{V}_E increases 15- to 25-fold, reaching 90 to 150 L/min (TV \\ge 3.0 L, f_b \\approx 35-45 breaths/min).

3. What is the ratio of minute ventilation to oxygen uptake called, and what is its normal value during low-intensity steady-state exercise?

A. Respiratory exchange ratio (RER); 0.70 to 1.00  
**B. Ventilatory equivalent for oxygen (\\dot{V}_E / \\dot{V}O_2); 20 to 25 L air per L O_2**  
C. Oxygen diffusion capacity; 100 mmHg  
D. Pulmonary clearance ratio; 5 to 10 L air per L O_2  

**Rationale:** The ventilatory equivalent for oxygen (\\dot{V}_E / \\dot{V}O_2) represents the efficiency of breathing, indicating how many liters of air must be ventilated to consume 1 liter of oxygen. At low-to-moderate intensities, it ranges from 20 to 25 L air / L O_2.

4. Why does the ventilatory equivalent for oxygen (\\dot{V}_E / \\dot{V}O_2) increase sharply to 35-40 L air / L O_2 during high-intensity aerobic exercise?

A. Respiratory muscles fatigue, reducing tidal volume  
**B. Ventilation increases disproportionately to exhale CO_2 generated from buffering metabolic lactic acid**  
C. Hemoglobin oxygen saturation drops below 50%  
D. Anatomical dead space expands to occupy 80% of tidal volume  

**Rationale:** At high intensities (above ventilatory threshold / OBLA), buffering of lactic acid by sodium bicarbonate produces excess CO_2, stimulating central and peripheral chemoreceptors to surge ventilation beyond oxygen demand to blow off CO_2.

5. What volume of the respiratory tract constitutes anatomical dead space in a healthy adult?

A. Approximately 50 ml  
**B. Approximately 150 ml**  
C. Approximately 500 ml  
D. Approximately 1,500 ml  

**Rationale:** Anatomical dead space comprises the non-diffusive conducting airways (nose, pharynx, trachea, bronchi) and averages approximately 150 ml in healthy adults.

6. Why is increasing tidal volume more effective for enhancing alveolar ventilation than increasing breathing frequency during moderate exercise?

A. Higher breathing frequency damages alveolar capillaries  
**B. Increasing tidal volume reduces the proportion of each breath wasted on anatomical dead space**  
C. Anatomical dead space collapses at higher breathing frequencies  
D. Breathing frequency has no impact on minute ventilation  

**Rationale:** Because anatomical dead space (~150 ml) is fixed per breath, a larger tidal volume delivers a higher percentage of fresh air to gas-exchanging alveoli ($V_A = (TV - V_D) \\times f_b$), whereas shallow rapid breathing wastes a higher fraction on dead space.

7. What is the partial pressure of oxygen in active muscle mitochondria during maximal aerobic exertion compared to resting arterial blood?

A. Resting arterial P_O_2 = 40 mmHg; Maximal muscle P_O_2 = 100 mmHg  
**B. Resting arterial P_O_2 = 100 mmHg; Maximal muscle P_O_2 = 3 mmHg**  
C. Resting arterial P_O_2 = 150 mmHg; Maximal muscle P_O_2 = 75 mmHg  
D. Resting arterial P_O_2 = 46 mmHg; Maximal muscle P_O_2 = 46 mmHg  

**Rationale:** Arterial blood entering capillaries has a $P_{O_2}$ of ~100 mmHg. During maximal exercise, intense oxygen consumption in active muscle cells drops tissue $P_{O_2}$ down to ~3 mmHg, creating a steep partial pressure gradient that drives rapid oxygen diffusion.

8. What is the partial pressure of carbon dioxide (P_CO_2) in active skeletal muscle tissue during maximal exertion compared to rest?

A. Rises from 20 mmHg at rest to 40 mmHg at max  
**B. Rises from 46 mmHg at rest to approximately 90 mmHg at max**  
C. Drops from 100 mmHg at rest to 10 mmHg at max  
D. Remains constant at 40 mmHg regardless of intensity  

**Rationale:** At rest, tissue $P_{CO_2}$ is ~46 mmHg. High metabolic production of carbon dioxide during intense exertion elevates muscle $P_{CO_2}$ to ~90 mmHg, creating a large gradient for $CO_2$ to diffuse into capillary blood.

## Concept Part 3: Blood Transport of Gases and Metabolic By-Products (p3)

1. How much oxygen is dissolved in each liter of arterial blood plasma under normal physiological conditions?

A. Approximately 0.3 ml O_2 / L  
**B. Approximately 3.0 ml O_2 / L**  
C. Approximately 30.0 ml O_2 / L  
D. Approximately 200.0 ml O_2 / L  

**Rationale:** Dissolved oxygen in plasma accounts for approximately 3.0 ml O_2 per liter of arterial blood. Although this represents less than 2% of total blood oxygen content, it establishes the partial pressure of arterial oxygen (P_aO_2) that drives hemoglobin saturation and tissue diffusion.

2. What is the oxygen-carrying capacity of one gram of pure hemoglobin when fully saturated?

A. 0.50 ml O_2  
**B. 1.34 ml O_2**  
C. 3.50 ml O_2  
D. 20.0 ml O_2  

**Rationale:** Each gram of fully saturated hemoglobin binds exactly 1.34 ml of oxygen (1.34 ml O_2 / g Hb).

3. What is the normal total oxygen content per 100 ml of arterial blood in a healthy male with a hemoglobin concentration of 15 g/100 ml?

A. Approximately 5 ml O_2 / 100 ml  
B. Approximately 12 ml O_2 / 100 ml  
**C. Approximately 20 ml O_2 / 100 ml**  
D. Approximately 35 ml O_2 / 100 ml  

**Rationale:** Total arterial oxygen content is calculated as (15 g Hb/100 ml \\times 1.34 ml O_2/g Hb) \\times 98% saturation + dissolved O_2 \\approx 20 ml O_2 per 100 ml of arterial blood (20 vol%).

4. What physiological shift in the oxyhemoglobin dissociation curve occurs in active skeletal muscle during exercise due to elevated temperature, increased P_CO_2, and decreased pH?

A. Leftward shift (Bohr effect), increasing hemoglobin affinity for oxygen  
**B. Rightward shift (Bohr effect), decreasing hemoglobin affinity for oxygen to enhance unloading**  
C. Upward shift, increasing total hemoglobin concentration  
D. Complete flattening of the curve, halting oxygen dissociation  

**Rationale:** The Bohr effect describes a rightward shift of the oxyhemoglobin dissociation curve triggered by elevated temperature, high $P_{CO_2}$, and low pH ($H^+$ accumulation) in active muscle, decreasing hemoglobin's affinity for oxygen and enhancing $O_2$ unloading into working tissue.

5. What is the primary pathway for carbon dioxide (CO_2) transport in human arterial blood, accounting for approximately 70% of total CO_2 carried?

B. Bound directly to iron molecules in hemoglobin

**Rationale:** Approximately 70% of carbon dioxide is transported in the blood as bicarbonate ions ($HCO_3^-$), produced inside red blood cells via the carbonic anhydrase reaction ($CO_2 + H_2O \\rightleftharpoons H_2CO_3 \\rightleftharpoons H^+ + HCO_3^-$).

6. What red blood cell phenomenon balances electrical charge when bicarbonate ions (HCO_3-) diffuse out of erythrocyte cytoplasm into blood plasma?

A. Sodium surge  
**B. Chloride shift**  
C. Potassium efflux  
D. Calcium influx  

**Rationale:** As bicarbonate ($HCO_3^-$) diffuses out of red blood cells into plasma, negatively charged chloride ions ($Cl^-$) move from plasma into red blood cells to maintain electrical neutrality, a mechanism known as the chloride shift (Hamburger phenomenon).

7. Approximately what percentage of carbon dioxide is transported bound to amino acids on hemoglobin as carbaminohemoglobin?

**B. 20%**  
C. 50%  
D. 70%  

**Rationale:** Approximately 20% of carbon dioxide is carried in blood bound to amino acid groups on the globin portion of hemoglobin molecules (carbaminohemoglobin), while ~70% is carried as bicarbonate and ~5-10% is dissolved in plasma.

A. Krebs cycle

### [sa]

1. **Clinical/Athletic Prompt:** Explain the chemical reactions and transport mechanisms involved in carrying carbon dioxide (CO_2) from active muscle capillaries to alveolar capillaries. Include the role of carbonic anhydrase, bicarbonate formation, and the chloride shift.

**Model Answer:** Carbon dioxide produced by active muscle cells diffuses into blood capillaries. Approximately 70% enters red blood cells, where the enzyme carbonic anhydrase rapidly catalyzes its combination with water to form carbonic acid ($CO_2 + H_2O \\rightleftharpoons H_2CO_3$). Carbonic acid dissociates into hydrogen ions ($H^+$) and bicarbonate ions ($HCO_3^-$). The $H^+$ ions are buffered by hemoglobin, while $HCO_3^-$ diffuses out of the red blood cell into plasma. To maintain electrical balance, chloride ions ($Cl^-$) move from plasma into the red blood cell (the chloride shift). Approximately 20% of $CO_2$ binds directly to amino acids on hemoglobin to form carbaminohemoglobin, and 5-10% remains dissolved in plasma. At the pulmonary capillaries, these reactions reverse: bicarbonate re-enters erythrocytes, converts back to $CO_2$ and $H_2O$, and $CO_2$ diffuses into alveoli to be exhaled.

**Evaluation Criteria:**
- Details the carbonic anhydrase reaction equation ($CO_2 + H_2O \\rightleftharpoons H_2CO_3 \\rightleftharpoons H^+ + HCO_3^-$) and bicarbonate transport (~70%).
- Explains the chloride shift mechanism ($Cl^-$ entering RBC as $HCO_3^-$ exits to balance electrical charge).
- Identifies carbaminohemoglobin transport (~20%) and dissolved plasma transport (~5-10%) alongside reversal at pulmonary capillaries.

2. **Clinical/Athletic Prompt:** Describe the physiological significance of the Bohr effect during intense exercise. Explain how local changes in temperature, P_CO_2, and pH alter hemoglobin oxygen affinity in active skeletal muscle versus pulmonary capillaries.

**Model Answer:** The Bohr effect represents a rightward shift of the oxyhemoglobin dissociation curve, which decreases hemoglobin's affinity for oxygen and facilitates $O_2$ unloading in active tissues. During intense exercise, contracting skeletal muscle generates high heat, elevated $P_{CO_2}$, and high $H^+$ concentrations (decreased pH). These local metabolic changes alter hemoglobin's molecular conformation, causing it to release oxygen more readily at any given tissue $P_{O_2}$. This ensures that working muscle mitochondria receive adequate oxygen despite brief capillary transit times. Conversely, at the pulmonary capillaries, blood cools slightly, $CO_2$ is exhaled, and pH rises, shifting the curve back to the left. This leftward shift increases hemoglobin's affinity for oxygen, maximizing $O_2$ loading in the lungs.

**Evaluation Criteria:**
- Defines the Bohr effect as a rightward shift of the oxyhemoglobin dissociation curve decreasing $O_2$ affinity.
- Identifies the three local muscle triggers: elevated temperature, elevated $P_{CO_2}$, and decreased pH ($H^+$ accumulation).
- Explains the physiological advantage: enhanced $O_2$ unloading at active muscle and enhanced $O_2$ loading at pulmonary capillaries.
